# Station audio

This is an audio-only overhaul. Damage, cooldowns, movement, collision, dash, Phase Cell timings, scoring, match duration, and the network protocol are unchanged.

## Sound design and sources

All gameplay sound effects are original procedural PCM generated by `client/audio-design.ts`. There are no external recordings, asset downloads, licensed sound packs, or audio-file dependencies. The underlying Web Audio API is native to the browser. No third-party audio licenses or attribution are required.

The bank is generated once after the first interaction and cached as mono AudioBuffers before playback. This provides essential combat sounds immediately without network loading or decoding. Four rifle variations and four footstep variations share a consistent design; rifle tuning varies by at most 1.4%. The generated bank adds a small amount of JavaScript instead of shipping uncompressed audio. The WAV in `artifacts/` is a test/review export only and is not loaded by the game.

| Event | Design / perspective |
|---|---|
| Pulse Rifle | A short broadband electrical onset, mechanical impulse, controlled 94/153 Hz body, and damped metallic reflections; 270 ms maximum buffer length with most energy near the onset |
| Other operators' rifles | Same family of sounds, positioned at the authoritative firing origin; progressively quieter, less detailed, and thinner in the bass with distance |
| Armor / target damage | Brief armor crack and electrical texture; the target additionally receives a centered low mechanical damage cue |
| Shooter confirmation | A quieter, dry double mechanical tick distinct from the target's damage sound |
| Arena impacts | Bright, short metallic snap for walls, floor, ceiling, and pillars; denser, duller impact for the central composite cover; no impact for an empty ray endpoint |
| Elimination | Armor/core fracture with a short fading electrical tail |
| Footsteps | Boot impact, slight sole contact, and restrained metal resonance; four variations, with cadence based on authoritative distance traveled |
| Dash | Powered onset and air displacement, followed by a short stabilization sound at the confirmed endpoint |
| Phase Cell | Quiet positional electrical presence, contained pickup surge, subtle holder texture, and a power-down cue on expiry; phased fire has a faint extra electrical layer |
| Station | Low ventilation and machinery, sparse positional creaks 17–25 seconds apart, and a modest ambience increase in the last 30 seconds |
| UI / state | Discreet join, match start, last-ten-second timing pulses, last-three-second respawn timing pulses, respawn, contract completion, and connection-loss cues |

There is no added pre-match countdown. Timing pulses follow the existing match and respawn timers. The later original background score uses a separate Music bus; see [MUSIC.md](MUSIC.md) for assets, adaptive behavior, and listening limitations.

## Space and mix

`client/audio-mixer.ts` uses world-space Web Audio PannerNodes with equal-power panning. The listener follows camera position, yaw, and pitch. Distance controls level plus high-pass/low-pass filtering; the local rifle and personal HUD feedback bypass the panner. This prioritizes clear left/right direction on a flat arena. It does not claim headphone-specific HRTF elevation or front/back localization.

A ray against the existing arena boxes applies a modest gain reduction and low-pass filter when substantial cover intervenes. This is lightweight acoustic occlusion, not room acoustics simulation. Short reflection textures are baked into the generated effects; there is no expensive global reverb or artificial stereo widening.

There are at most 24 overlapping one-shot voices, plus six continuous sources. Important local actions and nearby combat can replace low-priority distant effects. Stolen voices have brief fades, with at most four retiring one-shot tails. Effects, Ambience, Music, and UI feed a shared compressor, a soft safety ceiling, and Master. Critical local cues briefly duck ambience. Default settings are Master 75%, Effects 80%, Ambience 35%, Music 40%, and UI 65%.

## Playback lifecycle

- Audio starts only after interaction. **Enable Audio** appears when a browser context needs a gesture; an unavailable audio device never prevents playing.
- Master, Effects, Ambience, Music, UI, and Mute persist under `nb-audio` in local storage. The old mute preference is migrated. Gain changes are smoothed.
- Footsteps require actual ground movement in consecutive server snapshots. Stationary/downed players, spawn teleports, and dash displacement do not produce walking sounds. The game has no jump/airborne mechanic.
- Dash audio follows an increased server-confirmed cooldown, not the Dash button. Rejected attempts are silent.
- Phase loops track current server ownership and expiry. Leaving, a completed match, a stale connection, or a hidden page stops continuous sounds. Hidden pages suspend the context and the next interaction resumes it.
- No missed effects are queued while audio is locked or muted. All existing visual feedback remains available.

## Verification and listening

Run `npm test`, `npm run build`, `npm run test:browser`, and `npm run test:audio`. The audio suite renders the actual mixer in Chromium's OfflineAudioContext, exercises production-browser controls, and holds Fire for at least 60 seconds through the normal client/server path. Reports contain signal levels, channel energy, voice limits, activation, persistence, and cleanup checks.

**The mix has not been listened to by ear in this environment.** Numerical tests cannot establish comfort, realism, fatigue, subjective source recognition, or perceptual localization. Physical iOS/Android audio routing, hardware speakers/headphones, and real-device performance also need listening/playtesting.

`artifacts/audio-review.wav` provides these listening segments (one second each unless noted):

1. 0–1 s: centered local rifle.
2. 1–2 s: nearby rifle to the right.
3. 2–3 s: distant rifle to the right.
4. 3–4 s: rifle to the left.
5. 4–5 s: rifle to the right.
6. 5–6 s: armor impact.
7. 6–7 s: metal impact.
8. 7–8 s: composite impact.
9. 8–9 s: Phase Cell pickup.
10. 9–10 s: phase power-down.
11. 10–18 s: four-shooter stress mix with impacts, confirmation, footsteps, and ambience.

The review export uses full bus volumes to expose differences and stress headroom. Normal game defaults are lower. Listen at a comfortable device level, then compare the in-game mix and repeat a one-minute firing session before signing off on fatigue and balance.
